New in 1.5

Three new tools, still one small icon

Everything below runs on your Mac with Apple's own frameworks. No accounts, no cloud, no extra apps to install.

🔎

Copy text from any image

Screenshots of error messages, a slide in a video call, a scanned receipt, text in a photo — get the words out and paste them anywhere.

  • From your history: hover any image clip and click the text button, or right-click ▸ Copy Text from Image.
  • From anywhere on screen: press ⌃⇧⌘T and drag over it. The text lands on your clipboard; the image is thrown away.
  • Private and offline: powered by Apple's Vision engine (the one behind Live Text). Languages are detected automatically, and columns and tables come out in reading order.

Keep Awake

Stop your Mac from dozing off during a long download, a presentation or a build — without another menu bar app.

  • One small switch at the top of the panel. Pick 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1, 2 or 5 hours, or until I turn it off.
  • ⌥-click or right-click the menu bar icon to toggle it instantly — the icon turns into a coffee cup while it's on.
  • Sensible extras: keep the screen on or let it dim, turn on at launch, and switch off automatically on low battery.

Notification control, app by app

When your iPhone is connected, every Instagram like and Messenger ping pops up on your Mac too. Now you decide which apps are allowed to interrupt you.

  • Three modes: Show all, Choose apps, or Hide all — one click from the panel, or a shortcut you set.
  • Every app, iPhone included. Apps appear in the list as their notifications arrive; switch off the ones you don't want. You can also add one by name.
  • Nothing is lost. Hidden notifications collect in the panel under Hidden notifications. Click one to copy its text. They're kept in memory only and forgotten when ClipMate quits.

Everything ClipMate does

Clipboard manager, snippet keeper, screenshot and text-capture tool, caffeine switch and notification filter — in one menu bar icon.

📋

Clipboard history

The last 10 clips, newest first, without duplicates. Copy something again and it moves to the top instead of appearing twice. Keep 3 to 12.

📎

Text, files and images

Copy a paragraph, a folder or a screenshot — all land in the same list. Files show their real Finder icon; images show a thumbnail and pixel size.

📌

Four pins

Your email, your phone number, that reply you type every day. Click the pin on any clip to keep it; click again to release it.

📷

Area screenshots

Drag over any area with ⇧⌘D. Save a PNG to the Desktop or copy it straight to the clipboard — your choice, remembered.

🔎

Text from images new

Pull the words out of any image clip, or drag over anything on screen with ⌃⇧⌘T.

☕

Keep Awake new

A switch in the panel keeps your Mac from sleeping for as long as you choose. ⌥-click the menu bar icon to toggle.

🔕

Notification control new

Hide pop-ups from the apps you pick — your iPhone's included — and read them later in the panel.

✂

Windows-style cut in Finder

Optional. ⌘X cuts files and ⌘V moves them, like on Windows. Off by default.

📤

Drag straight out

Drag any row into another app. Files and images drop as real files — into Finder, Mail, Slack or anywhere else.

Your data stays on your Mac

ClipMate has no network code at all — no accounts, no analytics, no uploads. Here's exactly what it keeps and what it can see.

💾

What it keeps

Your recent clips and pins, saved only on this Mac. Copied images are stored as PNGs and deleted as soon as they drop out of the history. Clear everything any time.

🧠

What it only remembers briefly

Hidden notifications are held in memory so you can read them in the panel — never written to disk, and gone when ClipMate quits. Only app names and your on/off choices are saved.

🚫

What it never does

Send anything anywhere, read your screen in the background, or ask for a permission before you use the feature that needs it.

Permissions, and why

PermissionWhenWhy, and what ClipMate sees
NotificationsNeverClipMate never posts notifications. Confirmations appear in the panel or a small on-screen message.
Full Disk AccessNeverNothing outside ClipMate's own storage is read.
Screen RecordingFirst screenshotFor screenshots and Copy text from screen. Only the area you drag over, only when you ask. macOS needs ClipMate reopened once after you allow it.
AccessibilityOpt-inOnly if you turn on Notification control or Finder cut & paste. Notification control reads each pop-up's app name, title and text to close the ones you've hidden. ClipMate asks for your consent first.
Automation (Finder)Opt-inFinder cut & paste only — reads the selected files and the open folder.

ClipMate is switched on in System Settings but keeps asking? macOS ties permissions to an app's signature, and each update looks like a new app — so that switch belongs to the old copy. ClipMate spots this and shows Fix Permission: it clears the old entry and asks again. Switch ClipMate on once more and you're done.

Install in two minutes

ClipMate is free and isn't sold through the App Store, so macOS asks you to confirm it once.

1
Download and drag to Applications

Open ClipMate.dmg and drag ClipMate onto the Applications shortcut. Updating? Choose Replace.

2
Open it — and allow it once

The first launch shows "Apple could not verify ClipMate…". That's expected for free apps that aren't registered with Apple — it isn't a problem with ClipMate. Click Done, then:

System Settings › Privacy & Security › scroll down › Open Anyway

Confirm with your password or Touch ID. You only do this once. (Right-click ▸ Open no longer works for this on macOS Sequoia and Tahoe.)

3
Read the privacy screen

ClipMate shows what it keeps and what each permission is for. Click Done — nothing is requested yet.

4
Press ⇧⌘E

The panel appears. Copy a few things and watch them land. Open Settings from the gear to turn on launch at login, choose your shortcuts, and set up notifications.

git clone https://github.com/ganeshparajuli11/clipmate.git

Questions

Why does macOS say "Apple could not verify ClipMate"?

Apple only skips that warning for apps registered through its paid developer program. ClipMate is free and open source, so macOS asks you to confirm it once via System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Open Anyway. You can read every line of its code on GitHub.

I allowed a permission but ClipMate keeps asking. Why?

macOS remembers permissions by an app's signature, and every update looks like a new app. The switch you see on belongs to the previous copy. Click Fix Permission when ClipMate offers it (or in Settings ▸ Privacy & Permissions), switch ClipMate on again, and for Screen Recording click Reopen ClipMate.

Does ClipMate read or store my notifications?

Only if you turn on Notification control, and only after you agree. It reads each pop-up's app name, title and text so it can close the ones from apps you've hidden. Hidden ones are kept in memory for the panel and forgotten when ClipMate quits. Nothing is ever saved to disk or sent anywhere.

Does it work with notifications from my iPhone?

Yes. When your iPhone forwards notifications to your Mac (iPhone Mirroring / Continuity), they appear in ClipMate's app list like any Mac app — switch off Instagram, Messenger or anything else. To stop an iPhone app reaching the Mac entirely, use System Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ Allow notifications from iPhone.

Why does a hidden notification flash for a moment?

macOS doesn't let one app block another's notifications before they appear, so ClipMate closes them the instant they show up — usually before they finish sliding in. Settings ▸ Notifications has a Send test notification button to try it.

Is my screen or the images I scan sent anywhere?

No. Text recognition uses Apple's Vision framework on your Mac and works offline. When you copy text from the screen, the captured image is deleted straight after the text is read.

Will Keep Awake drain my battery?

It keeps the Mac awake only while it's on. Set a time limit, let the display dim, or have it switch off automatically below a battery level you choose. It also stops the moment ClipMate quits.

How it works

For the curious. No database, no accounts, no background uploads.

Capture

A one-second timer compares NSPasteboard.changeCount — a single number. The clipboard is only read when it actually changed. Watching the clipboard rather than your keystrokes is why cuts are captured too.

Storage

Text and file paths live in UserDefaults. Images are PNGs in Application Support, pruned the moment they leave the history.

Text recognition

VNRecognizeTextRequest at the accurate level with automatic language detection. Fragments are regrouped into lines, top to bottom and left to right, so tables stay readable.

Keep Awake

An IOKit power assertion — the same mechanism as the caffeinate command. macOS releases it automatically if ClipMate quits, so your Mac can never get stuck awake.

Notification control

Through the Accessibility API, ClipMate watches Notification Center's pop-ups and presses their own Close button when the app is hidden — exactly what clicking ✕ does. The Notification Center sidebar and your history are never touched.

Pasting images

Copying an image back publishes PNG and TIFF on one pasteboard item. Preview reaches for TIFF, browsers for PNG — so a paste never silently fails.
